This ledger records every current fixed limit, truncation point, bounded queue, buffer capacity, and script budget. V2
must preserve, raise, remove, or expose each as an explicit admission rule with a structured diagnostic.

## Limit classes

- `Platform capability`
- `Configurable safety budget`
- `Lossy retention/presentation budget`
- `Warning threshold`
- `Implementation artifact to remove`
- `Unknown`

## Ledger

Entries use `LIMIT-NNNN` identifiers. Next free identifier: `LIMIT-0077`.

Pass 1 recorded the resource, its value, and its enforcement site for every constant its method reached. Pass 2 read the
enforcing code for the entries that gate Phase 0A, ran a second discovery method aimed at truncation sites rather than
constant names, and allocated identifiers for the two families pass 1 deliberately left unnumbered.
`Proposed V2 rule` remained blank throughout passes 1-2 because it is the output of ADR-0021, not an input to it.

**Pass 3 — the class-and-owner sweep — has now applied accepted ADR-0021 to all 74 entries that existed then.** Every entry carries a
terminal failure class, one of the seven configuration owners, a proposed V2 rule, and a diagnostic. **No entry is
`Unknown`**: the 19 that were have been resolved, and the resolutions are visible as rules rather than as a class name
alone.

**Passes 4 and 5 then read use sites rather than definitions, and four rows left `Classified` as a result.**
`LIMIT-0013`, `LIMIT-0014`, `LIMIT-0015`, and `LIMIT-0017` are disposed again as of `29c22ef4`. `LIMIT-0015`'s was a
matter of finishing its overflow read; the other three needed a rule ADR-0021 does not contain, because they are
bounded queues the **engine** feeds and ADR-0021 permits runtime overflow only for queues fed by external unbounded
input. [ADR-0038](../decisions/ADR-0038-engine-egress-queue-classification.md) supplies it and supersedes three named
ADR-0021 clauses — part 1's runtime-overflow paragraph, part 3's `LIMIT-0013` bullet, and the decision driver behind
it. It is `Proposed`, so all four egress rows — `LIMIT-0013`, `LIMIT-0014`, `LIMIT-0076` and
`LIMIT-0017` — carry their disposition at `Investigating`. **Three of them become
`Classified` when it is accepted** — a disposition's supporting evidence is an accepted decision, and this ledger
does not get to relax that for its own convenience. **`LIMIT-0017` does not**: its producer is off the render path, so ADR-0038's fourth condition applies and nobody has answered it. That leaves **75 of 76** classified at ADR-0038's
acceptance rather than all 76 — 72 today plus the three that close with it.

**What `Classified` means here, and what it does not.** The [register vocabulary](README.md) defines it as required
fields *and* disposition filled with supporting evidence. The disposition is the V2 rule, and its supporting evidence is
an accepted decision — ADR-0021's class semantics and ownership axes, cited per entry — not a measurement. **No value in
this ledger has been measured.** A `Classified` row states what happens when the limit is exceeded and who owns the
number; it does not assert that the number is right. Setting numbers stays with each owner: P00A-T005 for `HostProfile`,
and the relevant contract or ADR for the other six.

**The `HostProfile` numbers now exist**, in the `Draft`
[host profile and render limits specification](../specs/spec-host-profile-and-render-limits.md). It maps every one of
the 28 `HostProfile`-owned entries below to a successor field — a mapping that is total for the first time now that
`LIMIT-0014` is split and `LIMIT-0013` has left the owner — and gives each a value with a stated basis — queried,
derived, V1 carry-over, or chosen. Exactly **one** of those values is derived from measurement — `predicted_quantum_cost_ratio` — with two more
*chosen and anchored on* EVD-0003; an earlier revision of this paragraph said three were derived, which the
specification's own basis vocabulary corrected. It also picks up `LIMIT-0031`, whose owner here is `N/A — removed` but whose disposition creates a
profile field. This ledger stays authoritative for what exists in V1 and who owns it; the specification is
authoritative for the value.

**ADR ownership.** The *truncation question* for every row in the silent-truncation register below is owned by
**ADR-0021**, including the ones whose subject matter is not rendering: `LIMIT-0067` truncates at project load, not in
the audio callback, but the Phase 0A exit gate is what forbids accepting an undiagnosed silent truncation as baseline,
so ADR-0021 must decide it. A row may carry a second ADR only for a *different* question about the same resource —
`LIMIT-0013` and `LIMIT-0020` keep ADR-0027 for what the taps are for and who owns them, which is not the same question
as what happens when they overflow. No row carries two ADRs for one question.

Passes 1-2 stated this ownership rule in the preamble but left `LIMIT-0013` and `LIMIT-0020` carrying ADR-0027 alone, so
two of the register's five rows contradicted it. Both now carry ADR-0021 as well.

**Configuration owner is the second axis**, recorded in its own column since pass 3. ADR-0021 separates a limit's
failure behavior from who owns its configuration, because this ledger contains budgets that are not render-preparation
inputs at all. The sweep's distribution over the closed set of seven:

| Owner                    | Entries | Reading                                                                            |
|--------------------------|--------:|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| `HostProfile`            |      28 | Render preparation capacity — the only owner that participates in plan admission   |
| Node contract            |      12 | Capacities intrinsic to one node's DSP, declared into the `ResourceReport`          |
| Protocol contract        |       8 | Reply, message, and per-client caps owned by the surface that serializes them       |
| `N/A — removed`          |      10 | Entries that cease to exist in V2                                                  |
| Application settings     |       7 | Editor and session budgets, unrelated to any render plan                            |
| Domain/format contract   |       6 | Bounds that are part of a value's representation or identity                        |
| Job policy               |       5 | Bounds of one long-running render or analysis job                                   |

Two thirds of the ledger is therefore **not** a `HostProfile` concern, which is the finding that justified splitting the
axes: a single-axis model would have put 48 of 76 entries in a render-preparation input they have nothing to do with.

**Every entry now has a settled owner**, which was not true until ADR-0038. Three moves produced the table above.
`LIMIT-0015` was `HostProfile` until pass 4 read its use sites; it is a deferred-drop channel, so it joins
`N/A — removed`. `LIMIT-0013` follows it there, because its channel turns out never to be constructed outside its own
tests. And `LIMIT-0014` — the one entry that had **no** owner, because a single constant sized a GUI ring and an OSC
ring while ADR-0021 allows one owner per entry — is split by
[ADR-0038](../decisions/ADR-0038-engine-egress-queue-classification.md) part 4: the GUI half keeps `LIMIT-0014` under
`HostProfile`, and the OSC half becomes `LIMIT-0076` under the protocol contract that serializes it. The register is
now **76** entries: pass 4 added `LIMIT-0075` and the split added `LIMIT-0076`.

**Pass 4 also produced two false findings, kept here because of how they failed.** It reported `MAX_NESTING_DEPTH`,
`MAX_SOURCE_LEN`, and `SCRIPT_MAX_PARAMS` as having no ledger entry, concluding that 74 was an undercount; they are
`LIMIT-0037`, `LIMIT-0038`, and `LIMIT-0040`, at exactly those citations, and the search had been for the *constant
identifiers* while those rows name their limits in prose. And it reported `LIMIT-0043` as a silent truncation on the
strength of a `.min()`, without reading `ExpansionBuffer::push` — which rejects and counts, making the `.min()` a
defensive no-op.

**Both failures are the one the audit was convened to correct.** Its subject was that passes 1-3 read definitions
rather than uses; it then asserted an absence from a name search and a behaviour from a single line, twice. External
review caught both. The transferable rule is narrower than "read the use sites": **a claim about what code does is not
finished until you have read what feeds it, and a claim that something is absent is not finished until you have
searched for it the way it would be written.**

**The register is now eleven entries, and the last four were found by classifying or by reading use sites — never by
searching.** Only the original five carry ADR-0021 §3 dispositions. `LIMIT-0004`, `LIMIT-0024`, and `LIMIT-0021` fall
under their entries' class rules rather than under that record's site-by-site list, so none of the three triggers its
taxonomy revisit condition. **`LIMIT-0014` now has a class rule** — ADR-0038 parts 1, 2 and 4 supply the engine-egress
rule ADR-0021 lacked, the GUI and OSC rings are split, and this half is `Lossy retention/presentation budget` owned by
`HostProfile`. It is disposed but not yet *closed*: the (a) side still has no drop counter, which is the one ADR-0038
condition V1 fails. Four consecutive discoveries outside the register is itself the finding.

`LIMIT-0024` is the seventh site, and the third that no search found. `MAX_CHANNEL_SENDS` is 16
(`crates/synth_engine/src/synth_engine.rs:3763`); the per-block send loop breaks at `list.len() >= MAX_CHANNEL_SENDS`, so a channel authored
with more than sixteen sends renders the first sixteen that **resolve** and discards every resolved send after that,
every block, with no counter and no diagnostic. The break is tested before the disabled-send and missing-return-bus
filters while `push` happens after them, so a disabled or unroutable send earlier in the list does not consume a slot —
the bound is on resolved sends, not on authored ones. The comment at `:3289` says so in prose — which is what makes it a pass-2 miss as well: that pass searched
for `silently (cap|truncat|drop|ignor)`, and this comment says "are dropped". Its V2 disposition already refuses an
over-sent topology at preparation, so the taxonomy holds and ADR-0021's revisit condition is not triggered; what was
incomplete is the register, for the third time.

**`LIMIT-0043` belongs in the register too, for a reason the audit first got wrong and then understated.** It reported
the entry as a site by reading the `.min()` at `note_processor.rs:1318`; that is a defensive no-op, since
`ExpansionBuffer::push` (`:1518-1527`) rejects and counts on overflow. But the count reaches nobody —
`expansion_drops()` (`sequencer_engine.rs:1105`) has no caller outside its module — and that is exactly the criterion
under which `LIMIT-0013`'s disposition was withdrawn. **A drop nobody can observe is silent.** Tenth entry.

`LIMIT-0004` is the sixth site, and it was found by classifying rather than by searching. The render command accepts up
to 384 kHz (`crates/pertylizer/src/render/mod.rs:51`) while `SampleRate::MAX_SUPPORTED` — documented as "the engine-wide ceiling", and the
value real-time look-ahead buffers size themselves from — is 192 kHz. `SampleRate::new` validates only positivity, so
nothing rejects the difference. The limiter's look-ahead ring is `0.005 x 192 000 = 960` frames and its request is
`clamp(1, MAX_LOOKAHEAD_SAMPLES)` (`crates/synth_modules/src/effects/limiter.rs:26,68-70`), so a 384 kHz render silently delivers 2.5 ms of the
5 ms the parameter advertises. Nothing overflows and nothing is unsafe; the audio is simply not what the patch asked
for, with no diagnostic. Any other module that sizes from the ceiling has the same shape.

**This does not trigger ADR-0021's revisit condition.** That condition fires when a truncation is found that no class
rule covers, which would mean the taxonomy is incomplete. This one is covered: it is a `HostProfile`-owned range whose
rule is a refusal at job admission. What it shows is that the *register* was incomplete — the weakness both earlier
passes recorded about themselves — and it is a third argument for the executable probe ADR-0021 lists as follow-up.

**It is fixed in V1 rather than left for cutover.** `MAX_RENDER_SAMPLE_RATE` now derives from
`DeviceSampleRate::MAX_SUPPORTED` instead of restating it, so the two cannot desync again, and three tests pin the
result: the rate above the ceiling is refused, the derivation holds, and the render bounds' product still sits under
`MAX_RENDER_BYTES`. That last one is a consequence worth recording — with the ceiling halved, **no legal request can
reach the size guard any more**, so it becomes a backstop against the other three bounds moving rather than a check
that fires. The two measurement harnesses got the same ceiling, so a future baseline cannot silently be taken on
degraded DSP.

**Of the eleven, only the original five have an accepted disposition, and one of those five has since been withdrawn.**
[ADR-0021 §3](../decisions/ADR-0021-host-profile-and-admission-policy.md#3-disposition-of-the-five-silent-truncation-sites)
covers `LIMIT-0001`, `LIMIT-0013`, `LIMIT-0020`, `LIMIT-0056`, and `LIMIT-0067` — four changing behaviour, and
`LIMIT-0013` keeping runtime dropping "with its counters promoted out of OSC-only publication". **That last one is
withdrawn**: the pass-4 use-site audit showed the counters are published nowhere and the queue is engine egress, so the
disposition rests on a reading that does not hold. **`LIMIT-0013`'s replacement disposition is
[ADR-0038](../decisions/ADR-0038-engine-egress-queue-classification.md) part 3**, which supersedes that bullet: the
channel is dead code and is removed rather than sized.

The six later entries — `LIMIT-0004`, `LIMIT-0024`, `LIMIT-0014`, `LIMIT-0021`, `LIMIT-0043`, and `LIMIT-0017` — are **outside** that record's
site-by-site list. Four of the six — `LIMIT-0004`, `LIMIT-0024`, `LIMIT-0021`, and `LIMIT-0043` — are covered by their own entry's class rule, so none triggers ADR-0021's taxonomy revisit condition, but
none has the site-specific disposition the original five got. **`LIMIT-0014` and `LIMIT-0017` were not even that** —
both sat at `Investigating`/`Needs review` under a class that does not describe them — and ADR-0038 is the record that
fixes it, by defining engine egress as a direction and making the failure class a property of the payload rather than
of the ring. **The exit gate's "no unexplained silent truncation" clause is therefore not satisfied by ADR-0021
alone**, and ADR-0038 is what closes the gap; while that record is `Proposed`, the four egress rows carry its
disposition but keep `Investigating`, because this ledger's own rule is that a disposition's supporting evidence is an
*accepted* decision. What remains open beyond it is the register's *completeness*: a truncation that is
both unnamed and undocumented is invisible to all three passes, and closing that needs the executable probe ADR-0021
lists as follow-up work.
